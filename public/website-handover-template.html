What goes in a website handover document?

  1. Website at a glance. Live URL, platform, launch date, client owner and the agreed support contact.
  2. Accounts and renewals. Domain, hosting, CMS and connected services. Name the account and billing owners, renewal dates and whether invitations were accepted.
  3. Everyday content changes. Steps for this site's editor: change a page, replace an image, preview and publish. Include the location of your own walkthrough.
  4. Backups and maintenance. A named owner, verified backup schedule and retention, restore instructions and the outcome of a restore rehearsal.
  5. Help and support. The channel, dates and scope you already agreed with the client, plus what to include in a problem report.
  6. Files and open items. Locations of source files and approved assets. Give each remaining item an owner, target date and status.
  7. Handover meeting. Have the client sign in and practise an edit. Confirm the records together and save the reviewed guide where the team can find it.

Turn vague notes into something the client can use.

Instead of: “The client owns the domain.”
Record: “Domain is in the client business account. Client director owns billing and renewal reminders. Next renewal: [verified date]. Client confirmed access on [date].”

Instead of: “Backups are automatic.”
Record: “[Provider] runs [verified frequency] backups with [verified retention]. [Role] checks success. Restore steps are in [location]. Last rehearsal: [date and outcome].”

Instead of: “Email me if anything breaks.”
Record: “Use [agreed support channel]. The existing support arrangement covers [scope] until [date]. Include the affected page, what happened and what you expected.”

Does this transfer the website to the client?

The document records the handover. Account invitations, billing changes and platform transfers still need to be completed in the relevant services. Confirm the client can use their own access before closing the project.

For WordPress, check the actual assigned permissions against the official roles and capabilities documentation. For Webflow, check the official site-transfer requirements for the relevant Workspace and Site plans. Platform details can change; the client's real setup takes priority.

What should stay out of the guide?

Keep passwords, recovery codes, payment details and private sign-in links out of the document. Record account owners and invitation status, then transfer credentials separately through the method agreed with the client. Review the document before sharing it.
